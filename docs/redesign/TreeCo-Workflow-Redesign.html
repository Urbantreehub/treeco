TreeCo Workflow Redesign

A full assessment of the app as it is today, what the best-rated scheduling and quoting tools do, and three genuinely different ways to rebuild TreeCo around one idea: the quote is the job. Nothing you have today is lost. The common path just gets a lot shorter.

In short

Recommendation: Direction A ("Quote Desk") for the record, with Direction C's "Today" inbox as the home screen. Every job becomes one quote-first record with a price, a status stepper, the quote description, photos and a live activity feed. Leads and acceptances email both you and Ashley. The scheduler gets truck rows, people as chips, and leave greyed out. The original nine statuses come back as a forward-only stepper. Menus hide everything that isn't the next step.

Direction B ("Flow Board") is shown because you asked for drag-and-drop everywhere; it is the most visual but the least phone-friendly. All three share the same job record, scheduler and emails, so the choice is about the home screen, not a rewrite.

Your workflow, as the app should understand it

Everything in TreeCo exists to move a piece of work along one line. Two commercial portals (Spencers / Downer) feed the same line from the side; residential leads come from the website form, email and phone.

Lead comes in→ Quote visit on a Tue/Thu run→ Quote sent→ Accepted→ Scheduled on a truck→ Stump grind→ Done, to invoice→ Invoiced in Xero On holdDeclined

Josh (owner)

Does every quote visit and writes every quote. Needs to know instantly when a lead arrives or a quote is accepted, and to see a quote's price and description without opening the builder.

Ashley (office)

Books visits, chases quotes, schedules trucks, invoices, and confirms portal changes. Needs one place that says what to action now, and a scheduler that shows who is available.

Crews (Isuzu, Nissan)

Open the truck iPad, see today's stops, take Before / During / After photos, mark done. Should never see quoting, leads or office menus.

The app today, honestly

I ran the app in demo mode at desktop, iPad and iPhone sizes and read the code behind every screen. The good news: the pieces are all there. The problem is that they are spread over eleven navigation items and the most important object, the quote, is the hardest to see.

Eight findings

  1. Activity is buried and passive. The activity feed renders in exactly one place: inside stage 4 of the quote builder, collapsed by default. It only ever shows real "opened" events; sends, follow-ups, acceptances and comments are reconstructed from columns. It has no buttons. (QuoteActivity.jsx, rendered at QuoteBuilder.jsx:1888; only quote_events.kind='opened' is ever written.)
  2. The quote is invisible from the job. Opening a job shows address, phone, notes and the raw enquiry photos. The price is a button label; the description is not shown at all. Once the quote is sent the enquiry material is still the biggest block on screen.
  3. Three status lists compete. The original ten statuses are in the dropdowns (you asked for them back on 6 Aug), a trimmed seven-item order and a four-item "manual" list are still shipped as dead code, and a fourth set of "forward action" buttons appears under the badge. Users see two competing ways to change status on one screen.
  4. Nobody is assigned to a job. Scheduling writes a truck lane (resource_id) but never who is on it (schedule.assigned_to is always empty). The truck iPad works only because it filters by lane. There is no leave or availability anywhere in the data model.
  5. Lead notifications are split. Email leads notify office@ only; website-form leads notify josh@ only; acceptances go to office plus Josh only for residential; declines reach nobody's inbox but the office. All addresses are hard-coded in five edge functions.
  6. Many quotes per job, no canonical one. Four separate "best quote" heuristics exist in the code. Versions are snapshots taken only on accept/reopen, so you cannot see what changed between sends.
  7. The best quote-tracking screen is unreachable. The Sent Quotes page (opens, follow-ups, accepted, declined) is not linked from any navigation.
  8. Eleven top-level items, four of them for occasional tasks. Mulch, Tools, Marketing, Team and Chat sit beside Jobs and Calendar. On a phone, seven of them collapse into "More" anyway.

The ten patterns that kept recurring

1. One record, status moves it

The simplest, best-rated tools never make you "convert" a quote into a job. Acceptance just flips the status.

2. Status is set by events, rarely by hand

Sent, viewed, accepted, declined and expired come from what the client did. Overrides live in one menu.

3. One primary button, computed from status

Draft: Send. Sent: Follow up. Accepted: Schedule. Done: Invoice. Everything else goes behind "…".

4. Rows are trucks or crews, with an Unassigned lane

Drag onto a row to assign. Availability is shown in the grid itself (grey blocks), not on a report.

5. A tray you drag from

Only accepted work sits in it. Jobber, Tradify, SingleOps and ServiceM8 all do this.

6. Conflict feedback at the moment of assignment

Names turn red, free slots show white, equipment double-booking warns.

7. One chronological feed per job

Client opens, questions, sends, edits and crew photos in one timeline. ServiceM8's Job Diary and Jobber's Activity Feed are the models.

8. A "needs you" home, not a stats dashboard

Quotient's dashboard is a to-do list. Things 3 and Linear's Triage are the interface models.

9. Prompted follow-ups, never automatic

12 hours unopened, 3 days no reply, 14 days final. One click sends an editable template.

10. Few tabs, more panels

The 2025–26 redesigns of Jobber, ServiceM8 and Housecall Pro all replaced long scrolls with side-by-side panels and tabs, and cut pages.

The status list, restored and made honest

Your original nine statuses plus Declined come back, but as a stepper that moves forward, not a dropdown. Most steps are set by an event. Only four are ever chosen by a person, and those sit under the "…" menu.

New Lead · enquiry receivedset by form, email, phone entry Quote Visit Booked (was Quote Scheduled)set when dropped on a quote run Quote Sent · shows Opened ×N underneathset on send Accepted · to be scheduledset by client, or "accept on behalf" Scheduledset when dropped on a truck-day Stump Grinding · main work donecrew, from the work order Done, To Invoicecrew, from the work order Invoicedset when pushed to Xero / portal On Hold · side stateperson, via … Declined · side state, reason keptclient, or person via …

Why this fixes the "where is it up to" problem: today a job can sit in Quote Sent with the quote accepted, or in Scheduled with nothing on the calendar, because a person picks from a list. With events driving the steps, the stepper cannot lie. The one thing you keep control of is the exception menu.

Three directions

Each one is a different home screen and a different way of moving work along. All three share the same job record, scheduler and emails (next section), and all three keep every current page reachable from a "More" menu.

Direction A

Quote Desk: a mail-style list, with the quote open beside it

Left column is the list of quotes, grouped by what needs you first (new leads, accepted, sent and waiting, done to invoice). Click one and the full record opens on the right with activity always visible. This is how Quotient and Apple Mail work, and it is the closest match to the way you already think.

Best forJosh writing and chasing quotes all day. Ashley scanning "what's accepted" and "what's new" in one glance.
What moves work alongThe one primary button on the record (Send, Follow up, Schedule, Invoice). Drag a row onto the Schedule tab to book it.
TradeoffNo single picture of the whole pipeline; you see a grouped list, not lanes.
Pick this ifyou want the app to feel like Quotient did, with the schedule and Xero attached.
Direction B

Flow Board: the status list as lanes, with this week's trucks docked underneath

The restored statuses become lanes grouped into three phases: Quoting, Doing, Getting paid. Each lane shows its total. Drag a card right to move it on; drag an Accepted card down onto a truck-day and it is scheduled. This is the Fergus / Housecall Pro model, and the most literal answer to "make dragging and dropping the main thing".

Best forSeeing the whole business at once: how much is quoted but not accepted, how much is done but not invoiced.
What moves work alongDragging. Cards carry the price, the client, a question badge and the age.
TradeoffNeeds a wide screen. Eight lanes get thin on an 11" iPad; the phone shows one lane at a time with swipe-to-advance.
Pick this ifyou and Ashley live on the Mac and the iPad in landscape, and you want the board to be the meeting room wall.
Direction C

Today: an inbox of decisions, one button each

The home screen shows only the things that need a human decision right now: new leads (book a visit or decline), accepted quotes (schedule), questions (reply), quotes gone quiet (follow up or snooze), finished jobs (invoice). Everything that is moving on its own stays out of the way. This is the Things 3 / Linear Triage model, and it is what an office admin actually wants at 8am.

Best forAshley's morning. Josh on the phone between quote visits.
What moves work alongButtons. Each card has one obvious action; the job record is one tap deeper.
TradeoffYou do not see the whole pipeline here; it lives under "All jobs". Depends on good rules for what appears (the follow-up thresholds you already have).
Pick this ifsimplicity beats overview, and you want the phone experience to be as good as the desktop.

They combine. The recommended build is C as the home tab, A as the Jobs tab, and B's board as an optional view on the Jobs tab for the days you want the wall. That costs almost nothing extra because they share every component below.

Shared pieces

The job record: the quote is the job

Open any job and the first thing you see is the price, the status stepper, the quote description and the photos. The activity feed sits beside it and is where you reply to the client. The enquiry, site notes, client details, work order, job pack, text-the-client and the exception statuses all live under "…". Once the quote is sent the pre-quote material stops taking up space, but it is one tap away.

  • One quote per job. Editing a sent quote creates version 2 automatically and takes it offline until re-sent. The activity feed shows "Edited v1 → v2". This replaces the four "best quote" guesses in the code.
  • Copy to new quote creates a new job number for the same client and links back ("Copied from Job 0412"). That is how repeat work and variations are handled, exactly as Quotient recommends.
  • Old jobs are recallable from the client's page, each with its own number, quote, photos and activity.
  • Activity that does something. Questions from the client are answered inline. "Send follow-up" logs to the feed. Crew photos land in the feed with the truck name. Portal notes for Spencers / Downer land here too instead of a separate Actions page.

The scheduler: trucks are rows, people are chips, leave is grey

Trucks (and the grinder trailer) are the rows. Every staff member is a chip you drop on a truck for the week or for one day. Leave and sick days grey out the person, and the scheduler warns when a job needs more people than the truck has that day. The tray on the right only ever holds Accepted work and quote visits waiting for a run, so it stays short.

The two emails you and Ashley both get

One event, one line, one button. Sent to every address listed in Settings → Notifications (so adding Ashley, or a future estimator, is a settings change, not a code change).

Feature specs, in plain words

Notifications

A recipients list in Settings, stored in app_settings. All five edge functions read it. Instant email for: new lead (any source), quote accepted, quote declined, client question. In-app only for: opened, follow-up sent, crew finished. Optional daily digest.

At a glance

Wherever you land, the first two groups are New leads and Accepted, with counts and dollar totals. On the phone these are the top two cards. The Actions page merges into this.

Quote-first job

Job record leads with price, stepper, description, photos. Pre-quote material and client details collapse behind "…" once status passes Quote Sent (this rule already exists as showsQuoteReference; it just needs to hide more).

Staff, trucks, availability

New resources table (Isuzu, Nissan, Grinder) replaces the hard-coded lane list. schedule.assigned_to is finally written when you drop a person on a truck-day. New availability table for leave, sick and part days. Truck iPads keep working unchanged.

Activity feed

quote_events gets written for sent, followed up, edited, accepted, declined, comment, photo, scheduled, invoiced (today only "opened" is written). The feed becomes the single source for Sent Quotes, the dashboard follow-ups and the drawer.

Versions and copies

Auto-snapshot on every edit after send (today only on accept/reopen). "Versions" control appears at v2. "Copy to new quote" creates a linked job. Accepted quotes stay locked; a variation is a new quote.

Menus, not pages

Primary nav becomes three or four items per role. Mulch, Tools, Marketing, Team, Chat, Clients and Settings live in More. Every status exception, text-the-client, work order and job pack live in the record's "…".

Access levels stay

Full, Office, Truck and Crew logins keep their fences. Trucks see the day run only; crew see safety, chat and their docs; Office sees everything but the money dashboard.

  • Touch drag activates after a 250ms hold with 5–8px tolerance, so scrolling never turns into a drag. Every drop has a menu equivalent.

Roadmap

Ordered so each step is usable on its own and none of them breaks the truck iPads.

  1. Week 1: notifications and the feed. Recipients list in Settings; all lead and quote functions email it. Write every event to quote_events. Restore the ten statuses as the single list and delete the dead trimmed lists. Files: supabase/functions/notify-office, inbound-lead, book-quote, send-quote-email, quote-followup, frontend/src/config/statuses.js.
  2. Week 2–3: the job record. Replace the drawer with the quote-first record (stepper, price, description, photos, activity, "…" menu). One quote per job with auto-versions. Files: JobDetailPanel.jsx (rewrite), QuoteActivity.jsx, migration for quotes.job_id unique + version trigger.
  3. Week 4: home and navigation. Today inbox (from Actions + DashboardWorkload + DashboardFollowUps), Jobs list grouped by need, 3–4 item nav with More. Files: Layout.jsx, Actions.jsx, Pipeline.jsx, Dashboard.jsx.
  4. Week 5–6: scheduler. resources and availability tables; people chips; write assigned_to; leave blocks; merge Planner quote runs into the grid. Files: Calendar.jsx, Planner.jsx, new migration.
  5. Week 7: board view and polish. Optional Flow Board on the Jobs tab, dark mode pass, e2e specs updated.
