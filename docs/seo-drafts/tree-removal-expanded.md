## Intro

When a tree is dead, diseased, storm-damaged, or simply outgrowing its space, removing it safely takes qualified hands — not just a chainsaw and a good day. Every removal we carry out starts with a proper look at the tree: its lean, health and decay, the ground it stands in, the wind it catches, and everything within reach if a limb comes down. From there we choose the right method for the site rather than forcing one approach onto every job. It's careful, considered work, and it's the reason our clients trust us with trees they'd never tackle themselves.

We're a Wellington arborist team led by an arborist holding one of the highest arboriculture qualifications attainable, with 11+ years working the region's hills and tight sections. The same qualified arborist who quotes your job is the one who does the work — so the advice you're given on day one is the advice that's carried out on the day.

## When does a tree need to come out?

Removal isn't always the answer, and we'll always tell you when a tree can be kept. But there are times when taking it out is the safe, sensible call:

- The tree is dead, or declining beyond recovery
- Significant decay, fungal brackets or a hollowing trunk have made it structurally unsound
- Storm damage has left it split, hung-up or leaning
- Roots are lifting paths, cracking foundations or threatening drains
- It's simply in the wrong place — shading the house, crowding a build, or outgrowing a small section

Where pruning, crown reduction or remedial tree surgery could solve the problem instead, we'll say so. Reading the whole tree first — and being honest about what it needs — is the part of the job that matters most.

## How our process works

It's deliberately simple. You get a free, no-obligation assessment on site, where the qualified arborist looks over the tree, talks through your options, and leaves you with a clear written quote — no pressure, no surprises. When you're ready to go ahead, that same arborist and our crew carry out the work to the plan you agreed. Because the person quoting is the person cutting, nothing gets lost between the sales pitch and the saw.

## Tight-access and high-risk removals

Wellington's steep sections, narrow driveways and exposed hillsides make tree removal a genuinely technical job — and it's where our experience earns its keep. Where there's room to work, a controlled directional fell is the fastest, most cost-effective option; we assess lean, wind, escape routes and the drop zone before the cut so the tree lands exactly where we intend.

Where space is tight — near a house, over a fence, above a neighbour's garden — we switch to sectional dismantling, lowering each limb on ropes so nothing lands where it shouldn't. Removals close to power lines, roofs and outbuildings are routine work for us, carried out by a fully qualified, insured team with the right rigging and gear for the site.

## What's included

A removal with us is a finished job, not a felled tree and a mess left behind. Every removal includes:

- **Full clean-up** — we chip the brush on site, cut the timber to your preference, and rake the area so it looks, in our clients' words, as if we were never there
- **Green-waste removal** — chippings and debris taken away, or left as mulch if you'd like it
- **Stump options** — grind the stump below ground level to replant or lay lawn, or full stump-and-root removal where you're paving or building over the spot

## Wellington-specific notes

A few things shape tree work in this city more than most:

- **Hill sections and access** — many jobs need roped dismantling rather than a straight fell, and gear carried in by hand. It's normal here, and we plan for it.
- **Wind** — Wellington's exposure means storm-damaged and wind-thrown trees are common, and a tree that looks fine can carry hidden tension. We assess the load before we release it.
- **Council consent** — some trees are protected under the Wellington City District Plan or sit on a notable-tree register. We check protection status as part of your free assessment and can advise on, or assist with, consent where it's required.

## What does tree removal cost?

Every tree is different, so we quote each one on site — but as a rough guide, small, easily accessed trees typically start in the low hundreds, mid-sized removals commonly land in the several-hundred-to-low-thousands range, and large or high-risk trees on difficult hill access can run higher again. Access, size, species, health and where the debris has to go all move the figure. The only accurate number is a free written quote after we've seen the tree — and there's no obligation to proceed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need council consent to remove a tree in Wellington?**
Some trees are protected under the Wellington City District Plan or sit on a heritage/notable-tree register. We check protection status as part of your free assessment and can advise on or assist with consent where it's required.

**How much does tree removal cost?**
Cost depends on the tree's size, species, health and access. We provide a free, no-obligation written quote after assessing the tree on site, so you know the price before any work begins.

**Is felling cheaper than dismantling?**
Usually yes, because it's quicker — but it needs enough clear space to drop the tree safely. On tight or sloping sections we dismantle piece by piece instead. We'll recommend the right method for your site during the free assessment.

**Do you clean up and take the waste away?**
Yes. Every removal includes a full clean-up — brush chipped, timber cut to your preference, and green waste removed. We leave the area tidy, or leave you the mulch if you'd like it.

**Can you remove a tree close to my house or power lines?**
Yes. Tight-access and high-risk removals near buildings, fences and lines are routine for us. We use sectional dismantling and proper rigging, and our arborists are fully qualified and insured.
