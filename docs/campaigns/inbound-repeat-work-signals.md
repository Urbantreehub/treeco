Pulled from josh@urbantreeservices.net, Sep 2026. These are **unprompted** emails —
customers who came back on their own, with no marketing at all. They are the single
best argument for the mailing list, and the model for its tone.

## What this tells us

1. **The return interval is 1–3 years, not 12 months.** Every one of these is a
   customer who intended to come back and simply took a year or three to get around
   to it. A reminder is not an interruption — it is a nudge on an open door. This is
   why the 12–24 month segment (300 people) and 24–48 month segment (733) are the
   real prize, not the recent customers.

2. **They always name the specific tree and the specific job.** "our large magnolia",
   "the cypress tree you trimmed", "my olive trees", "the hedge you trimmed prior".
   Nobody writes "your arboricultural services". The outbound email must mirror this
   exactly — which is what `marketing_contacts.last_job_summary` exists for. A generic
   "time for your tree maintenance!" throws away the one asset we have.

3. **The trigger is a problem, not a season.** Rubbing on a window, annoying tenants,
   blocking light, a tree that's grown back. The copy should name the consequence of
   leaving it, not sell "pruning".

4. **Their language is plain and slightly apologetic.** "just wondering", "we were
   wondering if you could", "I've decided to do something about". Matching that
   register — a short, plain note from Josh — will outperform anything that reads
   like a newsletter.

5. **Olive trees and hedges recur most often** as genuinely annual work. Worth its own
   segment: these customers have a standing yearly need and currently rely on
   remembering by themselves.

## Copy implication

The highest-performing email here is almost certainly the least designed one:
a short plain-text note from Josh that says *"we pruned your magnolia in March 2023 —
it'll be about due"*, and nothing else. That is a near-exact echo of what these
customers already write to us, which is the strongest possible evidence it lands.
